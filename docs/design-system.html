MusiChat · Stage 3 · Design system v2

The logo already told us what to build.

Two people drawn as speech bubbles — one cool, one warm — with music between them and a broken ring holding them together. That mark is a diagram of the interface, so the whole system derives from it: you are warm, they are cool, and a live session renders in the arc between you.

Azure — them Vibing together Rose — you

The mark

Reading the logo as a spec

Four things in the mark became rules. The gradient arc is the product's most important colour; the left/right split decides which person gets which hue; the break in the ring is why the solo state uses a dotted thread; and the chat tail on the lower arc is why no bubble in the app needs one.

Azure is them

The cool figure sits on the left of the mark — and their messages sit on the left of the thread. Cool light is what's far away.

Rose is you

The warm figure is on the right, where your own messages land. Your bubbles, your badges, your focus rings.

The ring is togetherness

The logo's own arc, reused as --together. Live sessions only — play buttons, progress, the vibing ring. Nothing decorative.

Mint is presence

Online status, nothing else. Deliberately outside the brand palette so "online" never reads as "vibing".

Logo usage

Three asset gaps to close

The supplied files work on light backgrounds at large sizes. The product is dark and mostly small, so three variants are missing before Stage 6 can ship a favicon.

GapWhyNeeded
Dark-ground wordmark "Musi" is navy #1A1A3E and disappears on the app's #141128 ground. Only "Chat" would be visible. A knockout variant with "Musi" in #F2EFFB, keeping the gradient on "Chat".
Simplified favicon At 16–32px the full mark — ring, two figures, note — collapses into mush. This is the single most common branding failure at small sizes. A reduced mark: the note alone in the gradient, or two bubbles without the ring. Test at 16px before committing.
Maskable PWA icon Android crops icons to circles, squircles and rounded squares. A transparent edge-to-edge mark gets clipped. 512×512 with the mark at 60% inside a solid #141128 field, plus purpose="maskable" in the manifest.

Sizes to export: favicon.ico (16/32/48), apple-touch-icon.png (180), PWA 192 and 512, maskable 512, and a 1200×630 Open Graph card. Minimum wordmark width is 96px; below that use the mark alone. Clear space on all sides equals the height of the "M".

Colour

Brand hues, tuned for night

The logo runs at full saturation, which is right for a mark seen for two seconds and wrong for an interface read for two hours. The UI keeps the brand's exact hue positions and pulls saturation down a step. The ground is deep indigo rather than neutral black — it sits under both the blue and magenta ends without fighting either.

Tokens are named --you-* and --them-* rather than by colour. You cannot accidentally use the wrong one.

Accessibility. Rose and Azure differ in lightness as well as hue, so you/them survives colour-blindness — but colour never carries meaning alone. Bubble alignment, avatars and labels repeat every signal. Body text on Ink 800 measures 14.2:1. Coral is never used on its own: at #FF8A45 it fails contrast for text and exists purely as the gradient's warm terminus.

Typography

Answering to the wordmark

The earlier draft paired a high-contrast serif with the interface. With the real wordmark in hand that no longer holds — it's a heavy rounded geometric, and an editorial serif fights it. Gabarito takes the display role instead: geometric, friendly, slightly unconventional, and close enough in spirit to the logo to look related without imitating it. Figtree keeps everything operational.

Both are free on Google Fonts. This preview page can't fetch webfonts and falls back to system faces.

Motion is spent in three places: the vibing ring, which must look alive because it's what prompts a session; the player sheet, the app's signature gesture; and the moment two people sync — the emotional peak and the only earned celebration. Everything else moves once, at 140ms. All of it stops under prefers-reduced-motion.

Guardrails

What this system refuses to do

RuleBecause
The ring gradient is reserved for live shared sessionsIf it decorates ordinary buttons it stops meaning "together" and the colour system collapses into styling.
Rose is never the other personYou are always warm, they are always cool, on every device. It has to be learnable in one session without a tutorial.
Coral never appears aloneIt fails text contrast at #FF8A45. It exists only as the gradient's warm terminus, exactly as in the logo.
Mint is only ever "online"Presence and vibing must never be confusable at a glance.
Gabarito never drops below 20px or labels a controlDisplay weight at small sizes reads as noise and hurts legibility on a phone.
Only one thing on screen may animate continuouslyAmbient motion everywhere feels cheap and drains battery during long listening sessions.
Colour never carries meaning aloneAlignment, avatars and labels repeat every signal, so the app works for colour-blind users and in sunlight.
No bubble tails, envelope icons, or vinyl recordsBorrowed signifiers would make this read as a WhatsApp and Spotify clone — and the mark already carries a tail.
